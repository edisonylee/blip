## Positioning (one line)

A quiet camera that turns your group's day into a single video, designed for the moment it lands in someone's Instagram Story.

---

## Mood references (concrete adjacent worlds)

- **Apple Photos memory videos** — restraint, type-as-chrome, the artifact is the hero
- **Letterboxd app** — confident editorial sans, dark surfaces, dignified small type
- **Are.na** — group composition, monospaced accents used surgically, not decoratively
- **Polaroid Originals** — warm timestamps as ritual, not nostalgia
- **Spotify Wrapped (typography, not gradients)** — moments rendered as type compositions

Explicitly **not** in the reference set: BeReal (loneliness), Locket (gloss), Snapchat (slang/noise), TikTok-native UI (algo anxiety), Setlog-original (cuteness, c-pink, doodles).

## Principles (the load-bearing 6)

### 1. The artifact is the brand, not the app
Every decision optimizes for how the exported video looks frozen as a Story cover.

**Why:** Instagram Story is the destination — the cover frame is the most-seen image of this brand per day.
**How to apply:** If a chrome element competes with the video, it loses. The app is a chrysalis.

### 2. Group is rendered, never described
Avatars, presence, posted-status — show, don't summarize.

**Why:** The moat against BeReal is making aloneness structurally impossible to feel inside the app.
**How to apply:** If a screen says "3 of 5 posted" without showing the 5 faces, it's wrong.

### 3. The grid is the visual identity
The split-screen multi-cell layout of the export is also the home screen, the app icon, the loading state, the share-sheet preview.

**Why:** One geometry repeated at every scale gives the brand a silhouette. People should recognize a Setlog Story from across the room.
**How to apply:** When in doubt, ask "could this be cells?" If yes, make it cells.

### 4. Time is typography
Hours, timestamps, countdowns are primary visual elements — set in one carefully chosen face, large enough to feel deliberate.

**Why:** This is where Lane A earns "modern" without going generic — precision around time. It also reclaims the timestamp-as-detail DNA from Setlog-original without inheriting the lowercase/monospace baggage.
**How to apply:** Time gets type weight; everything else gets out of its way.

### 5. Slight life, no animation noise
Things breathe — slots fill softly, presence dots pulse — but nothing bounces, slides, or celebrates.

**Why:** The app is calm *because* the day is busy. Hype reads as desperation in a passive-cadence app.
**How to apply:** Sub-200ms easing curves, not springs. Motion acknowledges state changes only — never entertains.

### 6. Dark, but warm
Near-black surfaces (not pure #000), a single warm accent in the cream/amber family. Color used once per screen, not as a system.

**Why:** The video is the color. Anything else competes.
**How to apply:** If a screen needs a second accent to feel alive, the layout is wrong.

## Signature moves (the things that make it not generic)

- **The 2-second pulse.** The capture control doesn't say "Record" — it pulses for 2 seconds and stops. The whole brand fits in that gesture.
- **Hour as cell.** Every cell in any grid carries its hour label in the same type style as the export. Hour-marks are a brand element, in-app and in-video.
- **Member tint.** Each member gets a subtle warm hue auto-assigned on join. Their video cells carry it as a 1-pixel border or corner mark. You learn the group's colors without being told.
- **Wordmark only at the end card.** "Setlog" appears once per day, on the closing frame of the export. In-app chrome never shows the wordmark — the app is confident enough not to brand itself to its own users.
- **Story-first frame.** The export's opening frame is composed to read as a Story cover. Treat it like an album cover, not a title card.

## Open questions (carry into core system)

1. Warm accent — concrete pick: warm-cream (~#F4E4C1), warm-amber (~#E8A87C), or somewhere else?
2. Type face — SF Pro is the iOS default; consider one editorial display face for time-as-typography (e.g. a confident geometric or a warm grotesque).
3. App icon — does the grid-as-identity principle resolve as a literal 2×2 of cells, or something more abstracted?
4. Member tint palette — how many distinct hues before they start to read as similar? (Probably 6–8.)
